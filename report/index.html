Why piccolo-gorgone was slow, and what gpu-share changed

piccolo-gorgone was decoding at 10–14 tok/s because cvoiced held 2.4 GB of VRAM, which forced ollama to put 15 of its 33 layers on the CPU. A new broker on ollama's port now makes cvoiced give the card back before any model load and protects live calls. It also serves piccolo through the turbo3 llama.cpp fork at a 262k context.

1. The cause, measured

Same prompts, temperature 0, ollama q8_0 KV at 64k context. The only change is whether cvoiced was resident. piccolo alone at 64k uses 6454 MiB, so the two never fit together at any useful context.

Ollama's own logs from the previous 14 days agree: 33/33 layers ran at ~40 tok/s, 25/33 at 8.3, and 18/33 at 11.5. Ollama evicts its own models when it needs room, but it cannot evict another process, and nothing else on the box could either.

2. The turbo3 fork: more context, at a price

"turbo3 cuda fork" is ~/data/llama-turbo3: Madreag/turbo3-cuda (from TheTom/llama-cpp-turboquant), forked to BogdanStamenovic/turbo3-cuda with a CUDA 13 build fix. It was already built here. Its speed and quality had never been measured.

Read this as a screen, not a verdict. These numbers come from the fork's own kl_divergence.py: top-10 logprobs on the next token, over 60 wikitext-2 prompts of ~2k tokens. An f16-vs-f16 rerun gives exactly 0, so the gaps are real and not noise. Still, n is small, the context is short, and top-k is an approximation. What they say: turbo3 is the only setting that reaches 262k, at ≤5% speed cost, but its error is about 10× that of q8_0 and worse than plain q4_0.

Because of that, there are two names. piccolo-gorgone:9b serves turbo3 @262144, which is what he asked for. piccolo-gorgone:9b-q8 serves q8_0 @131072, twice the old 64k and near-lossless. Which name is the default is his decision, and changing it is one config line.

Dead ends: q4_0-K + turbo3-V does fit 262k, but it deadlocks during prefill (GPU at 0% for 10 min). q8_0-K + q4_0-V runs only on a slow path.

3. What was built

clients (OpenHands on the tailnet, local agents, hotline-calld)

        │   └── everything else ────► ollama (moved to 127.0.0.1:11435)
        ├─ before a load: snapshot VRAM; evict cvoiced first, then idle LLMs
        ├─ call lease from hotline-calld: 5.1 GB reserved from ring to hang-up
        └─ every 5 s: poll call health, stop an idle llama-server,
           evict an ollama model left split across CPU and GPU
  • cvoiced yields by exiting through the new POST /yield (cvoice a9b5f5d). It waits for open requests and websockets, frees all 2408 MiB, and systemd brings it back empty at 0 MiB in 6.8 s. /unload is not used because it leaks ~780 MB per cycle.
  • In the request path, not a watcher. Ollama picks its layer split at load time, so the yield has to happen before the load. A watcher on /api/ps only sees the split after it has already happened.
  • Calls outrank LLMs. During a lease, a load that doesn't fit beside the call's budget is held rather than split, and gets a 503 after 120 s. The call may evict an in-flight LLM; an LLM never evicts another LLM's in-flight request. hotline-calld (c8d3720, manager-6) sends begin/renew/end; a health poll backs that up.
  • It degrades rather than fails. Without nvidia-smi, requests are forwarded unmanaged. Without llama-server, routed models fall back to stock ollama.
  • On the ownbox shelf. gpu-share and cvoice both install, update and remove through ownbox with the full lifecycle, and each was exercised live. gpu-share-frontdoor on|off moves ollama behind the broker and back.

4. What it costs calls

A yielded cvoiced has to load again: 0.8 s for the process plus 8.0 s for the model. A fully cold /speak takes 6.0 s; a warm one takes 0.4 s. hotline-calld already sends /load at ring time, so a call answered after about 9 s of ringing hears no delay. An inbound call answered automatically at 1.5 s may hear a few seconds of silence before the first word, if an LLM had the card just before.

5. Limitations

  • Ollama-native /api/chat and /api/generate for piccolo still go to stock ollama (64k, q8_0), because no protocol translator exists yet. OpenHands uses /v1, so it gets turbo3.
  • llama-server returns reasoning_content where ollama's /v1 returned reasoning. litellm reads the former.
  • Only calls are protected. A batch cvoiced user (kiri-wake, standin) that loads while an LLM holds 7.7 GB falls back to CPU: slow, but it still works.
  • One llama-server profile runs at a time; switching costs 2–5 s. Restarting the broker (including during ownbox update) drops any in-flight turbo request.
  • turbo3 at 262k leaves ~440 MiB spare at peak, so -np > 1 will not fit.
  • VRAM needs for ollama models are estimated until each has been seen fully loaded once.
